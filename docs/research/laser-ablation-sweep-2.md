# Second sweep lifts five metals off NONE

**BLUF.** The second literature pass adds **B-grade removal anchors on six of the seven 1064 nm pairings, where the first pass had only stainless** — bronze CuSn12 (single-track, 16 ns, 40 W: ≈5 µm/pass, η ≈ 2.8e-3 mm³/J), copper CW004A (10 ns, 12 W: 1.4 mm³/min, η 1.95e-3), Ti6Al4V (Nd:YAG 200 ns, 20 W: ≈10 µm/pass, η ≈ 1.1e-3), A-36 carbon steel (SPI 70 W fibre at 46 ns: 0.96 mm³/min, η 1.0–1.6e-3), tool steel 1.3343 and stainless 1.4301 at 4 ns (2.2–2.3e-4 mm³/J), SAE 304 on a ns Nd:YAG (19.4 µm/layer at 16 J/mm², η 1.0–1.6e-3) and the absolute 316L numbers inside the JMMP 2020 paper already in Table R (14–15 mm³/min at 5.3 J/mm², η 2.2–2.3e-3), with aluminium's B figure (INO MOPA, η ≈ 7.9e-3) partial and in the µJ regime — plus two practitioner D rows on copper and brass-class coin blanks, and vendor C-partial bands from Laserax for aluminium and steel at 100 W. The striking result is that **every hatched-pocket ns-1064 figure on every metal with a measurement lands in a single η band of roughly 1–3e-3 mm³/J** (bronze, copper, Ti6Al4V, mild steel, stainless), with aluminium the only metal credibly above it (4–8e-3 from Laserax and INO); the 10–30 µm/pass marketing figures, the IPG "6.25 mm³/s" headline and the untraceable "32 µm/pass on brass" remain unsupported by any measurement. Brass itself, however, is still **NONE at hatched-pocket level**: its two best sources (Hribar 2022, Dondieu 2021) hold the numbers in figures and a thesis PDF this sandbox could not open, and the one practitioner coin figure does not name its metal. Aluminium has three peer-reviewed sources with exactly the needed tables (Williams 2014/2015, Leone 2020, Dondieu 2021), all openly hosted and all blocked here, so a manual download turns aluminium from D-partial to B. The 355 nm UV picture is confirmed: no hatched-area removal rate on any bulk metal in the marker regime exists in the retrievable literature; the only measured 355 nm rows are 10 Hz foil scribes (Nammi 2016) and a secondary-cited copper etch rate whose primary (Tunna 2001) is unread. Nothing below is a prediction for a Lumos Ultra; every number carries the machine it was measured on.

Conventions follow the existing baselines doc: E_DA = P/(v·h) in J/mm²; η in mm³/J, with 1e-3 mm³/J = 1 µm per J/mm²; d_pass = η·E_DA; E_p = P/f; peak fluence F0 = 2E_p/(πw²). Grades A–E, "X-derived" and NONE are exactly as defined there; derived values never outrank their weakest input, and the arithmetic for every derived number is shown once in Section 3.

## 3. Evidence by material

### 3.1 Brass and bronze — one bronze anchor, brass still locked in figures

The only absolute Cu-alloy number at 1064 nm ns is from **Ghadiri Zahrani et al. 2024** on CuSn12 bronze with a 16 ns, 40 W Light Conversion Carbide (a DPSS source, not a fibre) at 200 kHz, 22 µm spot, 1000–1400 mm/s single 2 mm tracks measured by confocal microscopy: **30.47 µm after six passes at 1.82 J/mm² and 22.14 µm at 1.30 J/mm²**, with depth rising "almost linearly from 1 to 6 passes" ([Machines 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1702/12/1/63)). B-derived once: 30.47/6 = 5.1 µm/pass and 22.14/6 = 3.7 µm/pass; η = 5.1/1.82 ≈ 2.8e-3 and 3.7/1.30 ≈ 2.8e-3 mm³/J; E_p = 40 W/200 kHz = 0.20 mJ; F0 = 2·0.2 mJ/(π·(11 µm)²) ≈ 105 J/cm². Three caveats are load-bearing: the paper's "energy density" uses the spot diameter as width, so η is a line-dose figure equivalent to a hatch equal to the spot, and a pocket hatched finer than 22 µm will not see the same number; it is a single track with pile-up rims and a start-point depth about twice the mid-point; and the fitted effective absorptivity falls from 0.09 (passes 1–5) to 0.045 (pass 6), a measured-by-fit sign that per-pass depth decays with depth even at 30 µm ([Machines 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1702/12/1/63)).

For brass proper, **Hribar et al. 2022** ran CuZn37 and 316L on the same SPI G4 20 W MOPA (70–240 ns, 38/57 µm spot, 20 and 45 J/cm², 20–220 kHz, 3×3 mm hatched pockets, Alicona volume), but every absolute MRR sits in Figures 2, 3, 9 and 10; the text yields trend constants only: MRR peaks at a repetition rate equal to or slightly above the waveform's f0, **brass ablation at 220 kHz is ≈3.9 % of MRRmax**, optimum overlap is 37.5 % for brass against 25 % for steel, and brass's MRR/roughness quality estimator peaks at "almost twice" steel's ([Nanomaterials 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-4991/12/2/232/htm)). The Heriot-Watt programme behind the JMMP 2020 316L paper also engraved brass CW508L at 100 W and 200 W, but those numbers exist only in Dondieu's 2021 thesis PDF, which the proxy refused ([ROS record](https://www.ros.hw.ac.uk/handle/10399/4534)); the group's trade article gives the only brass-specific finding in prose, **up to +40 % MRR from longer pulses at higher PRF at constant average power**, with the shortest, highest-peak-power pulses "very poor" ([Laser Focus World](https://laserfocusworld.com/industrial-laser-solutions/article/14221797/laser-engraving-plays-to-strengths-of-nanosecond-pulsed-fiber-lasers)). IPG's Application Note 12 plots brass among ten materials on one removal-rate-versus-power chart (20–100 W, 100 ns, 1 mJ) that would give a same-source brass:stainless ratio, but the values are an image ([IPG App Note 12](https://www.ipgphotonics.com/de/578/Widget/Anwendungshinweis+12%3A+Tiefgravieren+mit+Faserlasern.pdf)).

Practitioner brass evidence is thin and partly contradictory. Shellhopper's G-Weike G2 Pro 50 W (Raycus Q-switched, 210 mm lens) 3D-slice job on 1 × 20 mm coin blanks at 500 mm/s, 30 kHz, 90 %, 0.0504 mm pitch and 256 passes reached "about 0.8 mm deep with decent detail" ([LightBurn t/166363](https://forum.lightburnsoftware.com/t/coin-engraving-settings-50-watt-gweike-fiber/166363)); D-derived once, 0.8 mm/256 ≈ 3.1 µm/pass and, taking 90 % as 45 W, E_DA = 45/(500·0.0504) ≈ 1.79 J/mm² so η ≈ 1.7e-3 mm³/J, ignoring the 32 cleanup passes and with the metal never named in the post. Thomasnomad's Cloudray 50 W on 0.5 mm brass sheet at 1200 mm/s, 80 %, 25 kHz, 0.01 mm claimed 0.25 mm in 12 passes one day and burned through the 0.5 mm sheet in the same 12 passes the next ([LightBurn t/83212](https://forum.lightburnsoftware.com/t/lightburn-software-bug/83212)); that is ≈21 to ≥42 µm/pass at E_DA = 40/(1200·0.01) ≈ 3.3 J/mm², i.e. η ≈ 6e-3 to ≥1.2e-2 mm³/J, two to five times every B figure, and the thin sheet, 0.01 mm pitch and day-to-day variance point to heat accumulation and melt ejection rather than steady ablation. It stays visible as a contradiction and is not a rate. Every other brass thread read (60 W JPT M7 strips, 60 W coins at 1800 mm/s/120 kHz/100 ns/256 slices, ComMarker B6's 512-pass recipe, WeCreat's own coin page) gives settings without a measurement; WeCreat's 0.01 mm per layer is a programmed Z-descent, not a measured depth ([WeCreat help](https://help.wecreat.com/?p=3918)). The "32 µm/pass, Raycus RFL-100M, 100 passes → 3.24 mm" figure was searched by its exact numbers and model and returned only machine listings; its arithmetic self-consistency is characteristic of marketing or generated text, and it remains E. Phosphor and tin bronze on any ns fibre: NONE. Zinc depletion after ns 1064 engraving of brass: NONE in text; the only composition data are 532 nm bronze oxidation (O rising to 12–17 wt%) ([Superficies y Vacío 2007](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/942/94220301.pdf)).

### 3.2 Copper — one B efficiency, two D practitioner brackets, no vendor number

**Žemaitis et al. 2021** (FTMC Vilnius) is the only peer-reviewed hatched-milling figure for copper with an ns 1064 nm source: CW004A 99.9 % copper, Ekspla Baltic HP at 10 ns, 100 kHz, 12 W, galvo snake hatch through an 80 mm f-theta, **1.4 mm³/min and 1.95 µm³/µJ** ([arXiv 2103.03687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03687)). The consistency check is 1.4/(12·60) = 1.94e-3 mm³/J, matching the stated efficiency, and 1 µm³/µJ = 1e-3 mm³/J. Spot, fluence, hatch pitch and passes are not in the text (the beam radius was varied to set fluence), so it is usable as an η but cannot be converted to a per-pass depth, and the source is DPSS rather than fibre. The same paper's ps and fs burst rows (2.0, 10.4 and 2.48 mm³/min) are kept as comparison only. The Bern 4 ns IPG YLMP-1 study gives the short-pulse, low-power floor for Cu-DHP: 0.019 mm³/(min·W) → 3.2e-4 mm³/J at ≤2.2 W, 50 kHz, 9 µm hatch, 450 mm/s ([Bern ARBOR](https://arbor.bfh.ch/bitstreams/4c4ab8a0-d4e8-464b-ba68-935d49c1e23a/download)).

The single-spot Nd:YAG literature is explicitly non-transferable. **Vlădoiu et al. 2008** (4.5 ns, 10 Hz, ≈1.1 mm spot, 20 pulses per crater) give copper a maximum ≈4.0 µm/pulse near 25 J/cm² and the fit Δh = 2.4 ln F − 4.2 above F_th ≈ 5.5 J/cm² ([JOAM 2008](https://joam.inoe.ro/articles/the-dependence-of-the-ablation-rate-of-metals-on-nanosecond-laser-fluence-and-wavelength/fulltext)); in η terms that is 4.0 µm/0.25 J/mm² = 16e-3 mm³/J, eight times Žemaitis, which is the signature of mm-scale spots at 10 Hz with melt expulsion, no plume shielding and no heat-accumulation limit. The baseline already holds this paper's threshold and δ; only the maximum rate and the fit are new. Jeleel & Hamoudi's 25 µm from one 9 ns pulse on 40 µm foil is a puncture datum, not ablation ([Iraqi J. Phys. 2018](https://ijp.uobaghdad.edu.iq/index.php/physics/article/download/26/19/36)). Lutey 2013's abstract names aluminium only, so the copper depths the baseline hoped for there do not exist ([UniBo CRIS](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/301517)); Hendow & Shakir's abstract supports only the direction that longer, lower-peak-power ns pulses remove more ([Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1364/OE.18.010188?fields=title,abstract,year,authors,citationCount)); Semerok 1999 stayed behind a bot wall.

Two forum reports carry a measurement. **JaredH** cut 0.8 mm copper sheet on a 60 W JPT MOPA (110 mm lens) at 95 %, 400 mm/s, 45 kHz, 190 ns, 0.005 mm line spacing, offset-fill inside a 0.03 mm slot, "through cleanly after about 30" of 40 passes in about half an hour ([LightBurn t/158872](https://forum.lightburnsoftware.com/t/copper-engrave-and-cut/158872)). D-derived once: 0.8 mm/30 ≈ 27 µm/pass; with 95 % of 60 W = 57 W, E_DA = 57/(400·0.005) = 28.5 J/mm² so η ≈ 0.027/28.5 ≈ 0.94e-3 mm³/J; E_p = 57/45 kHz ≈ 1.27 mJ. The narrow slot (edge effects, plume confinement, re-deposit) means an open pocket could sit either side of this. **Brad2** on a Gweike G2 50 W got "maybe 0.1 mm" in 16-gauge copper over 15–30 grayscale passes at 60–70 %, 30 kHz, 0.0254 mm and a speed he later found was mis-set at 38 mm/s, with a charred surface ([LightBurn t/163020](https://forum.lightburnsoftware.com/t/cutting-and-3d-engraving-copper-all-turns-black-and-it-will-literally-take-ages/163020)); that is 3.3–6.7 µm/pass at E_DA = 32.5/(38.3·0.0254) ≈ 33 J/mm², η ≈ 0.1–0.2e-3 mm³/J, five to ten times below JaredH and consistent with "too slow is charred, not deeper". Together the two D rows bracket 50–60 W MOPA copper at roughly 0.1–1e-3 mm³/J, at or below the stainless C/B band and below Žemaitis's optimised 1.95e-3. No vendor publishes a copper number with settings: IPG's Fig. 2 copper curve is unreadable and its "6.25 mm³/s" at 50 W (0.125 mm³/J) is 60× Žemaitis and 6× the top of its own chart axis, so it is a units slip or a soft material ([IPG App Note 12](https://www.ipgphotonics.com/de/578/Widget/Anwendungshinweis+12%3A+Tiefgravieren+mit+Faserlasern.pdf)); FLUX's Promark 100 W copper-coin tutorial is the only full MOPA vector for copper relief (50 %, 1000 mm/s, 0.0025 mm, 300 ns, 35 kHz, 100 layers) and reports no depth ([FLUX](https://support.flux3dp.com/hc/en-us/articles/14738844248079-3-6-Promark-Embossment-Engrave-2-5D-Low-Relief-Tutorial)). Copper back-reflection limits on 20–200 W ns fibre markers are not published anywhere found; every source advises tilt, off-centre placement and the source's own isolator ([Thunder Laser](https://www.thunderlaserusa.com/blog/can-you-laser-engrave-copper)).

### 3.3 Aluminium — the numbers exist, the PDFs are blocked

Three peer-reviewed sources contain exactly the aluminium tables DepthView wants, and none could be read here. **Williams, Brousseau & Rees 2014** milled Al 6082 with a 20 W SPI G3 MOPA (15–220 ns, 32 µm spot, 100 mm f-theta, 600 mm/s, 15–110 kHz, L9 DoE on overlap, track spacing and strategy) and measured MRR and Ra; the abstract says the frequency delivering the highest pulse energy and average power (PRF0, 51 kHz at 140 ns) gave the maximum MRR with high roughness, and the DoE cut roughness 60 % ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-014-6038-6)); the same work is Chapter 3 of the openly hosted Cardiff thesis whose PDF the proxy truncated ([ORCA 72916](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/72916/1/2015WilliamsEPhD.pdf)). **Leone, Genna & Tagliaferri 2020** milled 10 × 10 mm pockets in 2024-T3 with a 30 W Q-switched fibre laser and measured depth, Ra and MRR with a 3D profiler; its open-access ScienceDirect PDF is robots-blocked ([IRIS Unicampania](https://iris.unicampania.it/handle/11591/432242)). **Dondieu 2021** holds Al 5251 at 100 W and 200 W alongside brass and 316L ([ROS](https://www.ros.hw.ac.uk/handle/10399/4534)). These three are the highest-value manual downloads in this report; 6082 is the closest proxy to 6061 (both Al-Mg-Si).

What is readable is one B efficiency from a fibre MOPA in the µJ-pulse regime and one vendor band. **Gay et al. (INO) 2009** report a maximum ≈7.9 µm³/µJ (= 7.9e-3 mm³/J) on aluminium at 3.8 µJ and 20 ns with an 11 µm spot, against ≈1.1 µm³/µJ on 304 at 4.4 µJ and 30 ns on the same source ([INO SPIE 7386 PDF](https://bdt.semi.ac.cn/download/0.8961519607713108.pdf)) — the only same-rig Al:stainless η pair found anywhere, though at pulse energies two to three orders below a 60–100 W MOPA. **Laserax** quotes a 100 W fibre laser with a 254 mm lens removing 0.41–0.70 mm³/s on aluminium and 0.05–0.11 mm³/s on steel, dropping to 0.22–0.36 and 0.04–0.09 with a 420 mm lens, with preheated aluminium up to 40 % deeper ([Laserax](https://laserax.com/blog/deep-laser-engraving)); C-derived once, 0.41–0.70 mm³/s × 60 = 25–42 mm³/min and η = 0.41/100 to 0.70/100 = 4.1–7.0e-3 mm³/J. No settings beyond lens and power are given, so it is C-partial. Raycus's RFL-P200MX page states a pure-aluminium removal-efficiency ratio of **1 : 3 : 9.5 : 25.5 at 20/50/100/200 W** and captions one image "deep carving 12 s, depth 1.2 mm" without tying it to the 48 mm² pattern ([Raycus](https://en.raycuslaser.com/view/1877.html)); the exponents are ln3/ln2.5 = 1.20, ln9.5/ln5 = 1.40 and ln25.5/ln10 = 1.41 (E-derived), confirming the baseline's 1.4 while showing a gentler 1.2 at the low end, and the caption, if it were the 48 mm² area, would imply 4.8 mm³/s and 0.024 mm³/J at 200 W, which equals the single-pulse Vladoiu ceiling and is not seeded. ComMarker's blog gives aluminium a maximum depth of 2.43 mm at 100 kHz, none at 600 kHz, best at 0.01 mm hatch and 240 ns, with no speed, power, passes or time ([ComMarker](https://blog.commarker.com/archives/55878)); its "100 W vs 60 W, 148.97 vs 88.82 µm" pair is numerically identical to Cloudray's 304 test (148.968/88.817 µm, 15 passes) and is therefore a re-quote of that stainless test, not independent aluminium evidence ([Cloudray](https://www.cloudraylaser.com/blogs/machine-guide/what-is-the-difference-between-a-60w-and-100w-mopa-fiber-laser)).

The practitioner picture did not improve. Albroswift's 6061-T6 figure on a 60 W JPT M7 with a 175 mm lens ("about 10 thou about every 30 passes", ≈8.5 µm/pass) is unchanged; the new detail is the structure (36° hatch increment, 10 deep passes then ≈2 low-power "white" passes per block, which makes ≈10 µm per deep pass if the white passes remove nothing) and the statement that a 100 mm lens is "about 3 times" faster — matching the (175/100)² = 3.06 spot-area ratio, i.e. depth per pass scaling with fluence at fixed pulse energy ([LightBurn t/106751](https://forum.lightburnsoftware.com/t/deep-engraving-settings/106751)). The speed, interval, frequency and pulse width live in screenshots that need to be viewed as images. The single-spot Nd:YAG data carry an internal contradiction worth keeping: Vlădoiu 2008 reports aluminium at 1064 nm reaching ≈5.5 µm/pulse at ≈25 J/cm² (Δh = 2.8 ln F − 3.8, F_th ≈ 4 J/cm²) ([JOAM](https://joam.inoe.ro/articles/the-dependence-of-the-ablation-rate-of-metals-on-nanosecond-laser-fluence-and-wavelength/fulltext)), while the same group's CEJP paper reports ≤≈0.3 µm/pulse at 1064 nm over 6–20 J/cm² with 200 pulses per crater ([Stafe 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/243187144_Impact_of_the_lasr_wavelength_and_fluence_on_the_ablation_rate_of_aluminium)); the 2007 UPB paper with a ≈10 µm spot shows the mechanism (20-pulse fit 25 ln(F/25) µm collapsing to 5.4 ln(F/10) µm beyond 200 pulses, F_th rising from 4 to 25 J/cm² as the spot shrinks, and an abstract that disagrees with its body by 1000×) ([UPB](https://scientificbulletin.upb.ro/static/pdfs/full7955.pdf)). None of these are hatch-regime η; they bound it from above (≈2e-2 mm³/J for large-spot single pulses) and show the >20× efficiency collapse in deep, high-aspect craters that matters for coin relief.

### 3.4 Titanium — from NONE to one Nd:YAG anchor and a fibre band

**Campanelli et al. 2018** milled 10 × 10 mm squares 100 times each in 2 mm Ti6Al4V with a 20 W Q-switched Nd:YAG at 200 ns and 70 µm spot, over a 36-run factorial of 10/15/20 W, 300/500/700 mm/s and 10–40 kHz with hatch set equal to pulse spacing v/f, and measured depth by conoscopic holography validated against a CCI profilometer; depth reached "a maximum value near to 1 mm" at 20 W, 300 mm/s, 40 kHz (overlap ≈80 %), and Ra fell to ≈2 µm at 64–79 % overlap and below ≈20 J/cm² ([Micromachines 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-666X/9/7/324)). B-derived once: 1000 µm/100 passes ≈ 10 µm/pass; h = 300/40 000 = 7.5 µm; E_DA = 20/(300·0.0075) = 8.9 J/mm²; η = 10/8.9 ≈ 1.1e-3 mm³/J; E_p = 0.5 mJ; F0 = 2·0.5 mJ/(π·(35 µm)²) ≈ 26 J/cm²; one pass is 1333 lines × 10 mm / 300 mm/s ≈ 44 s so MRR ≈ 1.0 mm³/44 s ≈ 1.4 mm³/min. The "near to 1 mm" is a figure reading, so the row carries ±15 %, and it is Nd:YAG, not fibre, though the 200 ns pulse and 26 J/cm² are squarely inside MOPA deep-engraving territory. The depth optimum and roughness optimum nearly coincide (fine hatch, 300 mm/s, 40 kHz), which is the one design hint for titanium relief work.

**Perveen & Lutey 2020** ran Ti-6Al-4V, Hastelloy and Stellite 6K on a 20 W ns fibre laser (2–20 W, 20–80 kHz, 100–2000 mm/s, 1–20 passes) and found one optimum for all three, 20 W, 20 kHz, 1000 mm/s (1 mJ, 71 J/cm²), with **MRR 0.08–0.17 mm³/s** across the alloys attributed to liquid-phase ejection ([Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/offers.php?id=105664)); 0.08–0.17 × 60 = 4.8–10.2 mm³/min and η = 0.08/20 to 0.17/20 = 4.0–8.5e-3 mm³/J. At 1000 mm/s and 20 kHz the pulse spacing is 50 µm, i.e. essentially no overlap, which is why this melt-ejection band is three to seven times Campanelli's overlapped figure — for DepthView, titanium η is regime-dependent and must not be stored as one constant. The Ti-specific value is inside the paywall. Chandan & Sahoo's 40 W gas-assisted groove (1499 µm in 4 passes, ≈375 µm/pass) is a kerf with 25 L/min assist and no stated speed or frequency, an upper bound only ([CiteDrive](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/utilization-of-multi-pass-laser-processing-strategy-for-enhancing-the-capability-of-low-power-ns-laser-for-machining-of-ti-6al-4v-alloy/)); the 2024 Laser Technology groove array (140 µm deep at 24 W, 2000 mm/s, 0.01 mm) lacks a pass count ([jgjs.net.cn](https://jgjs.net.cn/article/doi/10.7510/jgjs.issn.1001-3806.2024.05.011)). Williams & Brousseau's CP-titanium crater and milling-efficiency data at 25–220 ns on a fibre laser sit in a CC-BY xlsx that was not opened ([Cardiff dataset](https://research.cardiff.ac.uk/converis/portal/detail/Dataset/1572910?lang=en_GB)). No vendor note with a measured titanium depth exists in anything found, and the one practitioner thread (100 W MOPA, 50 mm/s, 75 %, 100 kHz, 200 ns, 0.03 mm) posts settings without a depth ([LightBurn t/169766](https://forum.lightburnsoftware.com/t/deep-titanium-settings/169766)). Titanium colour versus depth at engraving doses: NONE.

### 3.5 Mild and tool steel — A-36 on a real SPI fibre, plus the 4 ns floor

The **COBEM 2023** A-36 paper is the cleanest new fibre-laser row in the sweep: SPI SP-070P-A-EP-Z-B-Y (70 W, 1 mJ max, 10–500 ns) at 46 ns and 10 kHz, ≈110 µm spot, overlap stepped 95 → 55 % with hatch equal to pulse spacing and pass counts 4–304, depth by Talysurf stylus, SEM morphology; its engraving test at 75 % overlap (275 mm/s, 27.5 µm pitch, 94 passes, focus 1 mm below surface) removed **8.5 mm³ over 69.81 mm² to 0.122 mm in 8.9 min, 0.96 mm³/min** ([COBEM 2023 PDF](http://www.sistema.abcm.org.br/articleFiles/download/40400)). B-derived once: 122 µm/94 ≈ 1.3 µm/pass; average power is not printed, and at 10 kHz the 1 mJ cap limits it to ≤10 W, so taking P ≈ 10 W (an assumption, flagged on the row) gives E_DA = 10/(275·0.0275) ≈ 1.3 J/mm² and η ≈ 1.0e-3 mm³/J from depth, or 0.96/(60·10) ≈ 1.6e-3 from the timed MRR (which includes scanner dead time, so the two bracket the truth); F0 for 1 mJ on 110 µm ≈ 21 J/cm². The paper itself cites the JMMP 2020 316L result at 10–12 mm³/min at 100 kHz for comparison. The low per-pass depth is a low-dose artefact (10 W, 275 mm/s, 27.5 µm), not a material property; the per-joule figure sits in the same band as 316L and Ti6Al4V. The per-overlap depth and per-layer volume are only plotted.

The Bern pulse-duration study gives tool steel 1.3343 (HSS, hardened 64 HRC and annealed) 0.014 and 0.013 mm³/(min·W) at 4 ns with the IPG YLMP-1 (≤2.2 W, 50 kHz, 18 µm radius, 9 µm hatch, 450 mm/s), identical to stainless 1.4301's 0.014, with thresholds 0.75 and 0.69 J/cm² ([Bern ARBOR](https://arbor.bfh.ch/bitstreams/4c4ab8a0-d4e8-464b-ba68-935d49c1e23a/download)); divided by 60 that is 2.2–2.3e-4 mm³/J, a short-pulse, low-fluence, quality-optimised floor roughly five to ten times below the 50–200 ns, 10–100 W figures, consistent with the baseline's depth ∝ τ^0.55 trend. Laserax's 100 W "steel" band (grade unspecified) of 0.05–0.11 mm³/s → 3–6.6 mm³/min → 0.5–1.1e-3 mm³/J is the vendor cross-check that 100 W ns steel removal is single-digit mm³/min, not the 10–30 µm/pass marketing figures ([Laserax](https://laserax.com/blog/deep-laser-engraving)). Saklakoglu & Kasman's H13 cavities on a 30 W fibre marker reach ≈195 µm at 200 mm/s, 0.02 mm, 60 %, 40 kHz with the pass count outside the abstract ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-010-2953-3)). No practitioner report with a measured mild-steel depth was found; lukes' 100 W JPT recipe (4000 mm/s, 75 %, 0.01 mm, 120 kHz, 350 ns, 80 passes toward 0.2 mm) shows photographs only, and if the target had been met it would mean 2.5 µm/pass at 1.9 J/mm² → 1.3e-3 mm³/J, an E-derived check that the circulating fast/fine recipes sit at 2–5 µm/pass, not 10–30 ([LightBurn t/167407](https://forum.lightburnsoftware.com/t/deep-engraving-text-steel-and-aluminium/167407)). 1018 and S235 specifically: NONE; A-36 is the only carbon-steel row.

### 3.6 Stainless — absolutes extracted, and a 1047 nm outlier to check

The first pass carried JMMP 2020 as an η band; this sweep recovered the absolute rows. With the SPI EP-Z G4 at a measured 108 W, 38 µm spot through a 160 mm f-theta, 13.3 µm hatch and 20 passes, **sequential engraving at 5.3 J/mm² and 280 ns removed 14 mm³/min at Sa 32 µm, interlacing (ΔIL 39.9 µm) 15 mm³/min at Sa 5 µm**, and the recommended engrave/clean/polish recipe (4.2 J/mm²/380 ns/175 kHz, 3.5 J/mm²/150 ns/350 kHz, 1.82 J/mm²/500 ns/500 kHz) gave 13 mm³/min at Sa ≈ 1.4 µm; MRR peaks near 200 kHz for 280/380 ns at 4.2 J/mm² and grooves collapse above ≈250/300/350 kHz for 500/380/280 ns ([JMMP 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-4494/4/4/110/htm)). B-derived once: η = 14/(60·108) = 2.16e-3 and 15/6480 = 2.31e-3 mm³/J; d_pass = η·E_DA = 2.16e-3 × 5.3 ≈ 11.4 µm and 12.3 µm interlaced; 1900 mm/s at 13.3 µm gives 108/(1900·0.0133) ≈ 4.2 J/mm², so ≈9–10 µm/pass at that dose. The interlacing gain (+7 % at 5.3, +20 % at 4.2 J/mm²) keeps the known contradiction with TRUMPF/SPI's "no MRR benefit on stainless" alive.

**Machines 2018** adds an independent ns source: a DMG MORI Lasertec 40 (lamp-pumped Nd:YAG, 30 µm spot, pulse width not stated) on 5 mm SAE 304, 4 × 4 mm pockets, 50 cross-hatched layers, hatch equal to pulse spacing, 126 combinations of 8/12/16 W, 20–80 kHz and 200–700 mm/s; **maximum layer thickness 19.4 µm at 16 W, 40 kHz, 200 mm/s** (7.8 and 12.9 µm at 8 and 12 W at the same speed and frequency), maximum removal 0.0261 mm³/s at 16 W, 20 kHz, 200 mm/s ([Machines 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1702/6/3/40)). B-derived once: h = 200/40 000 = 5 µm; E_DA = 16/(200·0.005) = 16 J/mm², so η = 19.4/16 = 1.21e-3, 12.9/12 = 1.08e-3 and 7.8/8 = 0.98e-3 mm³/J; from MRR, 0.0261 × 60 = 1.57 mm³/min → 1.57/(60·16) = 1.63e-3, 1.43e-3 at 12 W and 1.47e-3 at 8 W; the depth exponent from 8 to 16 W is ln(2.49)/ln 2 ≈ 1.3 and the MRR exponent ln(2.21)/ln 2 ≈ 1.15. This shows that a 19 µm layer on stainless needs 16 J/mm² per pass, which only slow, 5 µm-hatch recipes deliver; it also sits between Cloudray's linear 304 exponent (1.01) and Raycus's 1.4 on aluminium. Manninen 2015 (304, IPG 20 W, 4–200 ns, gravimetric MRR "increases significantly" with pulse length, floor cracking from ≥50 ns) remains abstract-only ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11663-015-0415-x)), but the same LUT group's open 316L drilling paper on the same laser family quantifies the direction: 200 ns/1 mJ/20 kHz drills 0.6 mm holes "over six times faster" than 8 ns/0.1 mJ/200 kHz at constant 20 W ([Appl. Sci. 2017](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/7/9/913)). The INO µJ-MOPA figure for 304 (1.1e-3 at 4.4 µJ, 30 ns) and Bern's 1.4301 floor (2.3e-4 at 4 ns) extend the pulse-energy and pulse-width axes downward ([INO](https://bdt.semi.ac.cn/download/0.8961519607713108.pdf); [Bern](https://arbor.bfh.ch/bitstreams/4c4ab8a0-d4e8-464b-ba68-935d49c1e23a/download)). Contuzzi & Casalino 2023 report 316L depths 15.6–60.1 µm and MRR 0.17–1.17 mm³/min over a 108-run Nd:YAG factorial but no laser power in the abstract, so no η ([Poliba](https://iris.poliba.it/handle/11589/253401)).

One figure contradicts the whole band and is flagged, not adopted. The UV sweep extracted from Byskov-Nielsen's Aarhus thesis a 1047 nm, 25–120 ns, 40 W study on stainless 1.4401 (47 µm waist, 20–29 J/cm²) with maximum volume removal "∼0.6 mm³/s" near 40 kHz ([Aarhus PhD](https://phys.au.dk/fileadmin/site_files/publikationer/phd/jeppe_byskov.pdf)); 0.6 mm³/s at 40 W is 15e-3 mm³/J, seven times JMMP 2020 and higher still if the power was below 40 W. Whether that figure is a peak single-line rate, a different definition or an extraction error cannot be settled from the note, so it is a verification target rather than a row to seed. Cloudray's 304 test is unchanged in substance (88.817 → 148.968 µm, 15 passes, exponent 1.01; d_pass 5.92 and 9.93 µm) but the sweep recovered its duration (2.43 min each) and its "pulse energy density" pair (447.88 vs 746.46 mJ/mm²), which, if the M7 60 W emits its 1.0 mJ maximum, implies a ≈53 µm spot and ≈60 kHz — an inference, not a published setting ([Cloudray](https://www.cloudraylaser.com/blogs/machine-guide/what-is-the-difference-between-a-60w-and-100w-mopa-fiber-laser)). The IPG "6.25 mm³/s" headline (0.125 mm³/J at 50 W, ~50× the B band) and Cloudray's "up to 5–7 mm" remain E.

### 3.7 UV 355 nm — the first-pass NONE stands, with foil scribes as the only measured rows

After roughly thirty targeted searches the conclusion is unchanged: **no peer-reviewed 355 nm ns paper gives a settings vector plus a measured hatched removal rate on bulk copper, brass, aluminium, stainless, titanium, gold or silver in the galvo-marker regime** (3–30 W, 20–200 kHz, 10–50 ns, 20–50 µm spots). The nearest primary is Tunna et al. 2001 (Opt. Laser Technol. 33:135, copper at 1064/532/355 nm), known here only through Rebegea's thesis table quoting copper etch rates of "about 13 µm/pulse at 532 nm, 7 µm/pulse at 355 nm, 2 µm/pulse at 1064 nm" with no fluence, pulse width or spot ([Rebegea 2018](https://etheses.bham.ac.uk/id/eprint/8555/1/Rebegea18PhD.pdf)); the link to Tunna is a title match, not verified. Fiorucci et al. 2015 wrote grooves in bulk Ti6Al4V with a Coherent AVIA 355 at 25 ns, 10 kHz, ≈0.1 mJ, 32 µm waist, ≈26 J/cm², 1–30 mm/s, single pass, with confocal and SEM measurement, but the depths are in Fig. 3 only ([Fiorucci 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275674299_Surface_modification_of_Ti6Al4V_by_nanosecond_laser_ablation_for_biomedical_applications)); at 30 mm/s each point sees ≈10 pulses, and if the ≈10 µm design depth was reached in one pass that would be ≈1 µm/pulse — an inference, not a datum.

The measured 355 nm rows are **Nammi et al. 2016**, a 6 ns Nd:YAG at 10 Hz with an estimated 200 µm spot and 95 % overlap scribing 35 µm copper foil on polyimide and 150 µm aluminium foil: copper 12/21/5 µm single-pass depth at 355/532/1064 nm and 45 mJ, aluminium 20/20/10 µm at 4 mJ, with "below 25 mJ, 355 and 532 nm depths were similar" and a multi-pulse check of ≈1 µm per pulse on copper at 20 J/cm² ([Nammi 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309892652_Pulsed_Laser_Assisted_Micro-Scribing_of_Metal_thin_Films_in_Air_and_Underwater_Using_UV_Visible_and_Near-IR_Wavelengths)). Both foils are 25× or more the ns thermal diffusion length (≈1.4 µm for Cu per Vlădoiu's Table I), so they behave as bulk for depth purposes, but the regime is 10 Hz and 13–143 J/cm², with no heat accumulation or inter-pulse shielding. B-derived once: ≈20 pulses per point, so Cu ≈0.6 (355), 1.05 (532), 0.25 (1064) µm/pulse and Al ≈1 µm/pulse at 355 nm; for aluminium a parabolic channel of ≈160 µm width × 20 µm depth is (2/3)·160·20 ≈ 2.1e3 µm² per 10 µm step → ≈2.1e-5 mm³ per 4 mJ pulse → η ≈ 5e-3 mm³/J (≈4e-3 triangular). That η is seeded nowhere; it is an order-of-magnitude upper bound with two geometric assumptions and a ±50 % spot. Copper η cannot be derived because the copper channel width is not stated. The 355/1064 advantage on copper (≈2.4× Nammi, ≈3.5× Rebegea/Tunna) matches copper reflectivity 0.45 versus 0.97 and does not carry to the MOPA case, where the 1064 nm source has 10–17× the average power of a 6 W UV module. Vendor literature gives Keyence's un-settinged "0.0001 to 0.005 inches" (2.5–127 µm) while routing deep engraving to 30–50 W fibre ([Keyence](https://www.keyence.com/products/marker/laser-marker/materials/metal-marking.jsp)), and WeCreat's "around 0.6 mm on UV alone" is a cutting spec ([WeCreat](https://wecreat.com/blogs/knowledge/can-uv-laser-engrave-metal)); no forum or video with UV settings and a measured metal depth was found. Brass, gold and silver at 355 nm ns: NONE. Measured 355 nm ns thresholds on bulk metal: NONE this pass. A 355 nm-specific energy-balance treatment: NONE.

## 7. Cross-material ratios per joule — what can and cannot be said

Nothing found measures brass, copper, aluminium or titanium against stainless at identical settings on one ns fibre laser and publishes the numbers in text; the three documents that do (Hribar's figures, IPG's Fig. 2, Dondieu's thesis) are in Section 6. What can be said is weaker and comes in two kinds. Same-rig pairs exist at the edges of the regime: at 4 ns and ≤2.2 W, copper removes **1.36× stainless 1.4301 per watt** (0.019 vs 0.014 mm³/(min·W)) and tool steel 1.3343 equals stainless ([Bern](https://arbor.bfh.ch/bitstreams/4c4ab8a0-d4e8-464b-ba68-935d49c1e23a/download)); at 2–4 µJ and 20–30 ns, aluminium's best η is **≈7× that of 304** (7.9e-3 vs 1.1e-3 mm³/J) on one INO MOPA ([INO](https://bdt.semi.ac.cn/download/0.8961519607713108.pdf)); at 4.5 ns and mm-scale spots, Al : Ti : Cu per pulse is ≈1.0 : 0.96 : 0.73 at ≈25–30 J/cm² ([JOAM](https://joam.inoe.ro/articles/the-dependence-of-the-ablation-rate-of-metals-on-nanosecond-laser-fluence-and-wavelength/fulltext)); and on one 20 W SPI, brass's quality-efficiency is ≈2× steel's while brass's removal collapses to ≈3.9 % of its maximum at 220 kHz where steel's does not ([Nanomaterials 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-4991/12/2/232/htm)). None of these is at 50–100 W with 100–300 ns pulses.

Cross-paper comparison at deep-engraving doses gives a bracket, labelled by its weakest input and never a ratio to store: bronze single-track 2.8e-3 (B-derived), Ti6Al4V 1.1e-3 (B-derived), A-36 1.0–1.6e-3 (B-derived), copper 1.95e-3 optimised (B) and 0.1–0.94e-3 practitioner (D-derived), against 316L 2.0–2.3e-3 (B-derived, JMMP) and 304 1.0–1.6e-3 (B-derived, Machines 2018). Read honestly, **every hatched ns-1064 measurement on bronze, copper, titanium, mild steel and stainless falls within a factor of ~2–3 of one another per joule once each is run near its own optimum**, with copper's practitioner rows at the bottom and bronze's high-fluence single track at the top; aluminium is the one material credibly above the band (4–8e-3 from Laserax and INO, consistent with Raycus's superlinear power scaling and lukes' plan of ≈2.3× fewer passes than steel). The baseline's E-derived placeholders that scale brass, copper and aluminium down from stainless by cold absorptivity are therefore wrong in direction for aluminium and probably too pessimistic for brass and bronze at ≥20 J/cm², where the measured absorptivity-by-fit on bronze (0.09) and Hribar's quality estimator both point to Cu alloys at or above stainless per joule once above threshold; copper remains the one Cu-family material where the practitioner evidence sits below stainless. What cannot be said is any number for brass : stainless or Cu : stainless at MOPA settings, any Ti : stainless ratio from a shared rig, or any ratio that survives a change of pulse width, since the LUT >6× (8 → 200 ns) and Bern (4 ns floor ≈10× below 200 ns figures) results show pulse duration moves η by more than any material does.

## Conclusion

The second pass changes the shape of the database more than its size: the first pass had one measured metal (stainless) and placeholders; this one has measured anchors on six of the seven 1064 nm pairings, and they agree with each other closely enough — a 1–3e-3 mm³/J band for everything except aluminium — that the per-joule constant, not the material, is the thing to seed, with material and regime as modifiers. That convergence is itself the most useful finding for DepthView's seeding logic, because it means a user's first coupon on any of these metals can be checked against a narrow prior rather than a guess, and any result outside roughly 0.5–5e-3 mm³/J should trigger a regime question (pulse width, hatch, overlap, heat accumulation) before a material explanation.

The remaining gaps are access problems rather than literature gaps. Four openly hosted documents (Williams, Dondieu, Hribar's PDF, Leone) would convert aluminium and brass from D-partial and trend-only into B rows and give the first same-rig brass:Al:stainless ratio at 100 W; one chart read by eye (IPG Fig. 2) would add a vendor C row for five metals at once. UV 355 nm is different in kind: the marker-regime metals literature does not exist, so the project's first A-grade UV coupon will be the first datum of its type, and the foil and secondary-cited rows should be bundled only as context that says so.
